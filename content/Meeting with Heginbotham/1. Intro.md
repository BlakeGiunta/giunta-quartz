The third ground is **Rule 204 irregularity**, if the order is defended as a psych-evaluation order. Rule 204.1 requires good cause, controversy, and written specification of details; Rule 204.4 authorizes court appointment in family cases. The order you uploaded does not appear to operate as a conventional Rule 204 appointment/order, and instead seems to make possession contingent on an evaluation by an expert selected by the opposing party. That is at least a serious procedural objection.

Do I have a time limit to request variance from SPO? 1. **Request written reasons for the variance from standard possession under § 153.258.**\
If the court is going to continue a no-possession or supervised-possession regime, force written specificity.
